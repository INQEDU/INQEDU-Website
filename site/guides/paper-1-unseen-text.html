Paper 1: a method for the unseen text

What to do in the first ten minutes with a text you have never seen, so the essay has an argument before you start writing.

Paper 1 gives you a text you have never read and a guiding question. Standard Level students write on one text. Higher Level students write on two. Most weak scripts fail in the same way: they walk through the text from top to bottom and name techniques as they pass. A strong script decides what the text is doing first, then proves it.

1. Read for the situation before the style

On the first read, leave your pen down. Ask four plain questions. Who is speaking? To whom? About what? What does the writer want the reader to think, feel or do by the end? If you cannot answer the last one in a sentence, read again. Everything else in your essay hangs on it.

2. Find the turn

Almost every passage shifts somewhere. The tone hardens, the focus narrows, the speaker changes their mind, an advertisement moves from problem to product. Mark that point. It usually gives you your structure: what the text does before the turn, what it does after, and why the change matters.

3. Choose three or four features that carry the meaning

You are not paid per technique. Pick the features that do the most work for the purpose you identified in step one. For a non-literary text this may include layout, image and typography as well as language. For each one, be ready to say what it is, where it appears, and what it makes the reader think.

4. Write a thesis that answers the guiding question

The guiding question is there to help you. Use its key words. A workable thesis has two parts: what the text achieves, and the main ways it achieves it.

The writer presents the city as a place that has already claimed its people, using repetition and a speaker who can only observe.

5. Plan paragraphs around ideas

Each paragraph should argue one point about the text's meaning. Avoid paragraphs titled by device, such as a paragraph on imagery followed by a paragraph on tone. Organise by what the text is doing, and bring in whichever features prove it.

A note on time

Ten minutes of planning feels expensive in the exam room. It is the cheapest marks you will earn. An essay with a clear line of argument and three well-developed points will outscore a longer one that describes.
