Choosing a global issue for your Individual Oral

A good global issue is narrow enough to argue and visible in the actual words of both your texts. Here is how to test yours.

The Individual Oral asks you to examine how two of the works you have studied present a global issue of your choice. You speak for ten minutes and then answer questions from your teacher for five. The choice of issue decides how well those ten minutes go, and most students choose one that is far too wide.

Too wide, too narrow, about right

"Gender inequality" is a topic. You could speak about it for an hour without saying anything about your texts. A global issue needs to be specific enough that your two texts take a position on it.

  • Too wide: gender inequality.
  • About right: how domestic expectations limit women's ability to speak for themselves.
  • Too narrow: the treatment of one character in one scene.

Three tests

  1. Is it significant and felt in many places? The issue should matter beyond one country or one decade, and show up in everyday life.
  2. Can you point to it in the language? Open each extract. If you cannot underline the lines where the issue is present, the link is in your head and not in the text.
  3. Do the two texts treat it differently? The oral is more interesting, and easier to structure, when the texts approach the issue from different angles or through different forms.

Choose the extracts for what they let you analyse

Your extracts should be rich in choices you can discuss: imagery, structure, voice, or visual features in a non-literary text. A passage that states the issue outright often gives you less to say than one that shows it indirectly.

Give both texts equal weight

Split your time evenly. For each text, move between the extract and the wider work, so the examiner can see you know the whole and not only the lines in front of you.

Practise aloud

An oral is a spoken argument. Record yourself, listen back, and note where you describe instead of analyse. Do this at least twice before the real thing. Your notes should be prompts, never a script.
